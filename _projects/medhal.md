Hallucination, i.e., the generation of non factual content by AI systems, poses serious risks in medical contexts, where errors can directly affect patient outcomes. Yet current hallucination detection methods face significant limitations when applied to specialized domains like medicine.

We present **MedHal**, a large-scale synthetic dataset specifically designed to assess capabilities for and to train models on the task of hallucination detection in medical texts. MedHal incorporates **diverse medical text sources** and tasks covering both **intrinsic and extrinsic hallucinations**, and provides a substantial volume of samples suitable for training medical hallucination detection models. Training on MedHal yields a baseline model that improves over general-purpose hallucination detection approaches. This resource enables more efficient evaluation and training of medical text generation systems while reducing reliance on costly expert review, potentially accelerating the development of medical AI research.

**Authors**: Fabrice Lamarche, Gaya Mehenni, Neshat Elhami Fard, Odette Rios-Ibacache, Li Ming Wang, John Kildea, Amal Zouaq

**Published at**: The 5th Asia-Pacific Chapter of the ACL and 15th International Joint Conference on NLP (AACL-IJCNLP 2026)
